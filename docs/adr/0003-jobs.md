# ADR-0003: Jobs, their runs and where they come from

Date: 2026-11-01
Status: accepted

## Context

Build step 4 adds the two run-to-completion modes of "Containers": the Cron
job and the One-off job. ProjectMatch needs both: a TTL cleanup on a
schedule, and two long asynchronous tasks its Service starts with a
payload. Services (decisions 92, 93) already pin images by digest, keep
pull credentials sealed, and are applied by an operator from the control
plane's rows.

How others do it:

- Google Cloud Run Jobs: a Job resource holds the image and settings; each
  execution may override args and env; Cloud Scheduler starts it on a
  timetable.
- Render and Heroku: a one-off job or dyno runs an existing service's
  current image with another command; cron jobs are a separate kind of
  service.
- Kubernetes CronJob: by default overlapping runs are allowed, a failed
  run is retried six times, there is no deadline, and the schedule is read
  in the controller's time zone unless `timeZone` is set.

## Decision

1. **One resource, `…/jobs`.** A Job holds an image (pinned at deploy, as
   for a Service), `command`, `args`, memory, CPU, a timeout and an optional
   `schedule` with an optional IANA `timeZone`. A Job with a schedule is a
   Cron job. `POST …/jobs/{id}/runs`, with optional `args` and `env`,
   starts a One-off job from any Job, scheduled or not.
2. **The control plane starts a One-off job itself**, while the request
   waits: it creates the Kubernetes Job and answers with the run. An
   Account with a Hold starts none (402), as it creates no Resource; its
   Cron jobs go on running, like its other Resources.
3. **Runs live in Kubernetes only.** The API lists a Job's runs from its
   Kubernetes Jobs by label and reads their state live. A finished run and
   its pod are kept for 24 hours, so its logs can still be read; a Cron
   job keeps at most its last 3 successful and 3 failed runs.
4. **Fixed run policy.** No automatic retries (`backoffLimit: 0`); a Cron
   job never overlaps its previous run (`concurrencyPolicy: Forbid`, the
   due run is skipped); every run is stopped after the Job's timeout,
   default 1 hour, at most 24 hours; schedules are read in UTC unless the
   Job names a time zone.
5. **The CronJob is the template.** The Jobs operator applies one CronJob
   per Job, `suspend: true` when the Job has no schedule. A One-off job is
   a copy of that CronJob's `jobTemplate` with the run's `args` and `env`,
   owned by the CronJob, as `kubectl create job --from=cronjob/…` does. The
   CronJob carries the spec version of the row it was rendered from
   (`jobSpecVersion`); until it carries the current one the Job is
   `deploying` and a run is refused with 409.

## Consequences

- Every run, scheduled or started, uses the digest pinned at the last
  deploy: no registry call per start, and a registry outage does not stop
  runs. A new image needs a `PATCH` or `POST …/deploy`, as for a Service.
- A One-off job starts in seconds, not after an operator pass. The control
  plane needs `create`, `list`, `get` and `delete` on Kubernetes Jobs in
  Project namespaces, and `get` on CronJobs, cluster-wide.
- The pod rules (gVisor, Secrets, egress, placement, limits) are written
  once, in the operator. Deleting a Job deletes its CronJob, and
  Kubernetes' garbage collector removes its runs with it.
- Right after a Job is created or changed, a run is refused until the next
  operator pass (30 s at most). The CLI waits for `ready`.
- There is no history beyond a day, and nothing to build alerts or reports
  on yet. Rows for runs, written back by the operator, can be added later
  without changing the run API.
- A failing run is not retried and a slow Cron job skips its next run
  instead of piling up; retries or overlap can become fields of a Job later
  without breaking the API.
- The glossary gains Job; a One-off job is a run started through the API,
  a Cron job a Job with a schedule.
